# Investigation 001: UAE fit-out surplus exchange

## Research question

Can a digital service recover unused fit-out materials before they are classified and transported as waste, while fitting Dubai's segregation, recordkeeping and licensed-transport regime?

## Evidence ledger

1. The UAE Circular Economy Policy states that construction and demolition waste accounts for 70–75% of the total mass of solid waste produced in the UAE, estimates 10–15% of building materials are wasted during construction, and says most demolition material is landfilled without value recovery. It explicitly calls for reuse and recycling programmes and digital material tracking.
   - https://assets.u.ae/api/public/content/0ddeb9f0bfce4c2f83e12d0f88b95512?v=2b82e618

2. Dubai Municipality's 2023 circular instructs construction and demolition contractors to segregate materials at source, including concrete, rebar, wood, tiles and plastics; remove reusable doors, windows, aluminium, cables and metal sections; and maintain quantity records.
   - https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2024/12/12-10-1.pdf

3. Dubai's Al Sa'fat green building system requires most new buildings to divert at least 50% of construction and demolition waste by volume or weight from landfill through reuse or recycling.
   - https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2023/01/Al-Safat-%E2%80%93-Dubai-Green-Building-System-2nd-editionJan2023.pdf

4. Dubai Municipality's current technical guidance says segregation and recovery for reuse or recycling are not considered disposal, and it recognises wood, metals, glass and other recoverable materials as recyclables.
   - https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2026/06/29-Technical-Guidelines-on-Construction-Demolition-Waste-Recycling.pdf

5. Dubai Municipality requires licensed collection vehicles and RASID location tracking for construction and demolition waste transportation.
   - https://dmpmedia.dm.gov.ae/uploads/2026/06/3-CD-Waste-Collection-and-Transportation.pdf

6. A Khalifa University thesis published in 2026 specifically examines UAE surplus construction materials and proposes digital marketplace functions including collaboration, verification and compliance. This means a generic marketplace is not itself a novel thesis; implementation and market entry are the unresolved work.
   - https://khazna.ku.ac.ae/en/studentTheses/enabling-construction-circularity-through-digital-platforms-a-mar

7. A 2026 UAE stakeholder study reports that circular construction markets are constrained by policy alignment, procurement, segregation, fragmented governance, gatekeeping and market structure more than technical capacity alone.
   - https://tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15623599.2026.2712618

8. A UAE University study reviews national construction and demolition waste policy and argues for a 3R framework spanning site plans, procurement and building lifecycle decisions.
   - https://research.uaeu.ac.ae/en/publications/construction-and-demolition-waste-management-in-the-united-arab-e

## Synthesis

A generic listing marketplace is too shallow. The stronger wedge is a closeout workflow for fit-out contractors: identify unused surplus before disposal, preserve evidence that it remains a usable product, match it to a nearby project, and hand any regulated transport to an approved operator. The service should not initially collect, store, certify or transport materials itself.

## Proposed idea: FitLoop UAE

**Customer:** SME fit-out contractors, project managers and quantity surveyors handling commercial interiors in Dubai.

**Problem:** Surplus tiles, unopened flooring, lighting, sanitary fittings, doors, cable and packaged materials are visible late, fragmented across sites, poorly documented and often easier to discard than transfer.

**Product:** A mobile closeout workflow that creates a verified surplus manifest from photos, invoice/spec evidence, quantity and pickup deadline; searches nearby demand; generates transfer records; and requests quotes from approved logistics or waste operators when necessary.

**Business model hypothesis:** Monthly contractor plan plus a success fee on recovered value. No custody of funds or materials in the pilot.

**First validation test:** Interview ten fit-out project managers and quantity surveyors. Ask for anonymised examples from their last three closeouts, including material type, value, disposal decision, storage window and who had authority to transfer ownership. Do not build a marketplace until at least five can produce recurring unused surplus and two agree to test a manual matching service.

## Principal risks

- A material may become regulated waste once the owner declares it unwanted.
- Material certification, warranty and liability may stop receiving projects from accepting it.
- Pickup windows may be too short for marketplace-style matching.
- Procurement rules and consultant approval may matter more than price.
- Large contractors may already have internal redistribution channels.

## Open question for The Third Chair

At what exact operational or legal point does unopened surplus from a Dubai fit-out project become regulated waste rather than transferable inventory, and what documentation preserves its status as a usable product?

Useful responses need a Dubai Municipality source, contract clause, consultant procedure, or first-hand contractor example. Opinions without evidence do not reopen the investigation.
